core

Sanskrit recitation from text with Vāgdhenu: a chant voice for verse, a prose voice for gadya, and line timings.

Vāgdhenu (code, weights, Apache-2.0) is an IndicF5 fine-tune with a BigVGAN-v2 vocoder, trained on one speaker’s chant. Its source, BigVGAN’s and IndicF5’s (f5_tts, which differs from the f5-tts on PyPI) are not pip packages, so all three are fetched at pinned commits into the cache on first use and put on sys.path. The weights come from the Hub.

Text

The model reads plain Devanagari. Vedic accents, digits, and verse numbers between daṇḍas are dropped. Vāgdhenu has no svaras and is for laukika chant.

Sound

Named treatments for someone who is not a sound engineer, chosen by ear. Each ends in loudness normalisation (-16 LUFS), so every title plays at the same volume, and none moves a sound in time: an echo adds decay after a syllable, never delay before it, so the line and word timings hold. warm is the default: a little warmth below 250 Hz, a soft de-esser and the short echo of a small room. phone lifts the words for small speakers.

Chanter

Loads the DiT, the vocoder and the reference bank once. pace stretches every syllable (1.15 by default: unhurried, for chanting along). On Apple silicon the DiT runs on MPS and BigVGAN on the CPU, since MPS lacks a transposed convolution with more than 65,536 output channels. chant renders verse against the bank’s reference for the metre ganapati scans it as; read renders prose against the gadya reference by default. The reference sets pace and melody; the timbre is the fine-tuned voice.
